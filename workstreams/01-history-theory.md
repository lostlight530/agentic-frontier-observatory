# F1｜History, Theory, and Paradigms / 历史、理论与范式

F1 owns the observatory's long-range conceptual and historical frame. It asks whether a current AI claim is genuinely new, a recombination of older ideas, or a familiar limitation appearing under new scale/interface conditions.

## Core questions

- Which intellectual, mathematical, computational, or institutional lineage does the current development extend?
- What changed in assumptions, scale, data, compute, interfaces, deployment, or governance rather than vocabulary alone?
- Are competing definitions of intelligence, agency, autonomy, reasoning, memory, or learning being conflated?
- Does a current success address an older limitation, or only move the operating point?
- Which historical analogy is useful, and where does it break?

## Preferred evidence

Prefer original papers, standards/specifications, archival institutional records, first-party technical retrospectives, and well-scoped scholarly histories. Secondary histories can provide orientation but should not overwrite primary dates or original claims.

A retrospective source published later may clarify an earlier event while remaining a later interpretation.

## Analytical boundaries

```text
historical similarity != identical mechanism
shared vocabulary != shared capability
later interpretation != original contemporary belief
paradigm label != proof of discontinuity
```

Avoid teleological histories that make present outcomes look inevitable. Preserve abandoned branches, failed assumptions, and competing paradigms when they materially explain the current frontier.

## Handoff to F7

F1 contributes a bounded lineage: the prior concept/system, the current development, what actually changed, what did not, the dates/sources supporting that relationship, and any analogy limits.

F1 does not decide present-day deployment maturity by historical analogy alone.
