### English

The defining feature of AI in August 2026 is not merely another stronger model. Across seventy years, AI moved from symbolic rules and statistical learning to deep neural networks, foundation models, and generative interfaces. It is now entering an agentic-systems phase in which models, tools, memory, execution environments, protocols, identity, authorization, and governance are coupled.

The purpose of this observatory is therefore to track an emerging technical order rather than isolated products. MCP standardizes connections to tools and data; A2A addresses discovery, communication, and task collaboration between independent agents; and institutions such as NIST and the Linux Foundation are formalizing security, identity, open governance, and interoperability.

## 2｜Historical Continuity / 历史连续性

The seventy-year arc can be read as a sequence of changing bottlenecks:

```text
Rules → knowledge acquisition → data → representation learning → scale
→ instruction following → tool and environment access
→ interoperability, identity, authorization, recovery, and governance
```

Each transition preserved older methods rather than fully replacing them. Agentic systems still depend on symbolic interfaces, statistical learning, neural representations, software engineering, and human institutions.

## 3｜Critical Developments / 关键变化

### 3.1 Agent interoperability is becoming an institutional field

NIST launched its AI Agent Standards Initiative on 17 February 2026, explicitly organizing work around industry-led standards, community protocols, identity, authorization, security evaluation, and sector adoption.

### 3.2 Open governance moved beyond single-vendor ownership

The Linux Foundation created the Agentic AI Foundation on 9 December 2025 with MCP, goose, and AGENTS.md as founding contributions. This is a governance signal: important agent infrastructure is being positioned as shared ecosystem infrastructure rather than only as vendor product surface.

### 3.3 A2A reached a stable protocol layer

The official A2A documentation identifies 1.0.0 as the latest released version and describes discovery, interaction modalities, collaborative tasks, security requirements, streaming, and asynchronous work between independent agent systems.

### 3.4 Measurement remains behind capability

Stanford’s 2026 AI Index reports rapid gains and benchmark saturation while also identifying transparency and measurement gaps. For agentic systems, closed-loop tasks, environment differences, and long-horizon reliability make evaluation substantially harder than static question answering.

## 4｜Workstream Findings / 分任务发现

### F1 History and theory

The current agentic turn is best understood as a systems-integration phase, not as a clean break from earlier AI. It combines learned representations with symbolic contracts, software tools, external environments, and institutional control.

### F2 Models and algorithms

Model progress remains important, but model-only comparison increasingly under-describes deployed capability. A weaker model in a disciplined harness may outperform a stronger model in an unreliable environment for long tasks.

### F3 Infrastructure

Agentic systems shift cost and risk from training alone toward inference duration, tool calls, sandboxing, observability, storage, and recovery. Infrastructure becomes part of the behavior specification.

### F4 Agentic systems

MCP and A2A address different boundaries and should not be treated as direct substitutes. MCP centers model/application access to context, tools, and data; A2A centers communication between independent agent systems.

### F5 Evaluation and governance

Identity, authorization, auditability, and human escalation are no longer optional administrative details. They are becoming technical requirements for agents that act on behalf of users.

### F6 Ecosystem and society

Open foundations may reduce fragmentation, but neutral governance does not guarantee interoperability or safety. Adoption, SDK conformance, semantic compatibility, and operational incentives still require observation.

## 5｜Cross-System Analysis / 跨系统分析

A durable architecture is emerging in layers:

```text
Model capability
→ Harness and runtime
→ Tool/data access
→ Agent-to-agent communication
→ Identity and authorization
→ Evaluation, audit, and governance
```

The layers are related but not interchangeable. A protocol can define message exchange without proving task success; an SDK can implement a protocol without guaranteeing safe deployment; a benchmark can measure a workload without establishing general reliability.

## 7｜Stable Judgments / 稳定判断

- AI history is cyclical and layered, not a straight replacement sequence
- Product capability and open standards evolve at different speeds
- Protocol publication does not equal ecosystem interoperability
- Agent performance cannot be separated from its environment and authority boundary
- Human judgment remains necessary for source calibration and consequential action

## 8｜Uncertainties and Conflicts / 不确定与冲突

- No universal definition of an AI agent is accepted across research, industry, and policy
- Protocol compatibility does not establish semantic understanding between agents
- Vendor-reported long-horizon performance remains difficult to compare
- The relationship between decentralized discovery and enterprise registries remains unsettled
- Identity and authorization models are still fragmented across web, cloud, and agent-specific approaches
